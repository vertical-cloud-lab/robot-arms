# Visualizing Robot Positioning Accuracy and Repeatability in 3D

## 1. Overview

Three principal methods appear in the published literature for showing a robot's positioning accuracy and repeatability in three dimensions: **(1) error ellipsoids** (the stochastic ellipsoid method), **(2) probabilistic workspace maps**, and **(3) Jacobian-based confidence volumes along a path or trajectory**. Each can be computed from physical measurements, from modeled parameter uncertainties, or from both. ## 2. Error Ellipsoids (Stochastic Ellipsoid Method)

### 2.1 Mathematical Construction

The stochastic ellipsoid method represents positioning uncertainty at a given robot configuration as a three-dimensional confidence region derived from the covariance of end-effector position errors. Makhambetov et al. describe the procedure: "The displacement samples are calculated as Δp = p_noisy − p_nom, then their covariance matrix Σ is eigendecomposed as Σ = VDVᵀ. The eigenvectors in V define the principal directions of the uncertainty ellipsoid, while the eigenvalues λ_j represent variances along those directions. A 3σ ellipsoid uses semi-axis lengths a_j = 3√λ_j" (pqac-00000012). The volume of this ellipsoid, (4/3)π∏a_j, provides a scalar summary of total uncertainty while retaining directional information that a single RMS number discards (pqac-00000012).

Alternatively, the ellipsoid can be computed analytically from the kinematic Jacobian. Issametova et al. explain: "Robot parameter or drive-error uncertainties are represented by a covariance matrix Σ_q and propagated through the kinematic Jacobian, using the relationship Σ_X = J_q Σ_q J_qᵀ" (pqac-00000002). The 3×3 translational block of Σ_X is then eigendecomposed to obtain the ellipsoid. For task-space error, Sinha and Chakraborty (preprint) show that joint-space uncertainty propagated through the position Jacobian J_p yields "δXᵀ(J_p J_pᵀ)⁻¹δX ≤ c," which "geometrically describes an ellipsoid" of position errors at the end-effector (pqac-00000030).

### 2.2 Protocol and Validation

Issametova et al. validated the ellipsoid approach experimentally on a **FANUC LR Mate 200iD/7L**, measuring at "25 workspace points and 50 repeated trials per point, with a laser tracker accurate to about 0.01 mm" (pqac-00000015). They found that analytical and Monte Carlo ellipsoids agreed within 1–3% for compact and medium configurations, but diverged by 7–10% for elongated configurations where nonlinear kinematics dominate: "Experimental error ellipsoids showed that the major axis could be displaced by up to 10–11° relative to model predictions, with variance up to 20% higher than predicted" (pqac-00000039). This is a peer-reviewed journal article (DOI: 10.3390/math14030580).

Makhambetov et al. used a purely computational approach on a generic 6-DOF manipulator with "N = 10,000 trials across 50 workspace configurations" and rendered 3σ error ellipsoids through a Unity3D Digital Twin (pqac-00000031). Their ellipsoids were anisotropic, with the longest axis primarily vertical due to gravitational elastic deformation (pqac-00000014). This is a peer-reviewed journal article (DOI: 10.3390/electronics15143095) but contains no physical measurement validation; the authors note that "future work will compare them with measured end-effector scatter using a laser tracker" (pqac-00000019).

Rezala and Arbaoui used the stochastic ellipsoid method to analyze repeatability errors caused by joint friction variations, noting that "the stochastic ellipsoid method is able to generate repeatability predictions for the entire workspace" (pqac-00000035). This is a peer-reviewed journal article (DOI: 10.5829/ije.2022.35.02b.21).

### 2.3 How to Repeat for Low-Cost Arms

For the AgileX PiPER or SO-101, one can command the arm to the same pose repeatedly (≥30 cycles per ISO 9283), record the TCP position using an AprilTag-based external camera system or a dial indicator, compute the 3×3 position covariance from the scatter, and eigendecompose it to obtain the ellipsoid axes and semi-lengths. This requires no laser tracker; the user's existing fixed cameras and AprilTags can serve as the measurement system, though with lower absolute accuracy than a laser tracker. **No published study has applied error ellipsoid analysis specifically to AgileX PiPER, SO-101, or comparable sub-$2,000 arms.**

## 3. Probabilistic Workspace Maps

### 3.1 Monte Carlo Workspace Characterization

Workspace maps extend single-point ellipsoid analysis to the entire reachable space. Makhambetov et al. constructed a "probabilistic workspace map using 3,000 uniformly sampled configurations within joint limits and 500 trials per point, totaling 1,500,000 forward-kinematics evaluations" (pqac-00000012). The result is a color-coded 3D point cloud: "green denotes RMS error below 5 mm, yellow 5–15 mm, and red above 15 mm" (pqac-00000013). The maps reveal that "central workspace corresponds to compact configurations" with low error, while "reliability decreases toward workspace boundaries" (pqac-00000018).

Issametova et al. constructed a geometric-reliability map for the FANUC LR Mate 200iD/7L, defining reliability as the probability that the end-effector lands within a tolerance region. They report "approximately 0.98 in central workspace regions associated with compact configurations" declining to "approximately 0.70 in peripheral zones" (pqac-00000039). This was computed from both analytical (Jacobian-based) and Monte Carlo methods, peer-reviewed journal article (DOI: 10.3390/math14030580).

### 3.2 Measurement-Based Workspace Maps

Chen applied the ISO 9283 protocol to the **Baxter** robot to generate "a 3D mapping of repeatability at different heights," finding that "repeatability generally deteriorated as the target moved farther from the robot" (pqac-00000000). The protocol tested 15 target locations at three heights, with 30 cycles per experiment repeated 30 times (900 measurements per location), using 3D dial gauges with 0.005 mm accuracy (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000027). This is a thesis (2015).

### 3.3 ISO 9283 Protocol Summary

The ISO 9283 standard prescribes the canonical measurement protocol. A test cube is placed in the most-used workspace region with edges parallel to the robot base coordinates. Five poses (P1–P5) lie on a diagonal test plane, P1 at the cube center and P2–P5 near diagonal corners. The robot visits these poses in unidirectional order through 30 cycles at each of three speeds (10%, 50%, 100% of rated velocity) and 100% rated payload. Position repeatability is computed as "RP_l = mean distance from the barycentre plus three standard deviations" (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000023). This protocol has been applied with various measurement tools including laser trackers (Faro Vantage, accuracy ±0.009 mm), contact gauges, and vision systems (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021).

## 4. Jacobian-Based Confidence Volumes and Error Along a Path

### 4.1 The Probabilistic Product-of-Exponentials (PPOE) Model

Meyer et al. presented the PPOE model at IROS 2022, which "does not only consider the robot's deterministic geometry but additionally models time-varying and configuration-dependent errors in a probabilistic way" (pqac-00000028). The model propagates errors along the kinematic chain using Lie-group operations and computes their influence on the end-effector pose. It was validated on a **Kinova Jaco2** arm, where "PPOE-based 2D estimation achieved mean lateral errors of 0.29 ± 0.15 cm and 0.50 ± 0.25 cm" compared with forward kinematics alone at "0.82 ± 0.49 cm and 1.48 ± 0.38 cm" (pqac-00000042). This is a peer-reviewed IEEE conference paper (DOI: 10.1109/IROS47612.2022.9981399).

### 4.2 pytransform3d: An Open-Source Uncertainty Visualization Library

The Python library pytransform3d provides the most accessible implementation for low-cost arm users. Fabisch and Popescu describe its uncertainty module: it "models uncertain rigid transformations using a transformation-matrix mean and a 6×6 covariance matrix over exponential coordinates" and supports "composition, inversion, and fusion of uncertain transformations" (pqac-00000008). Key functions include `concat_locally_uncertain_transforms(A2B, B2C, cov_A, cov_B)` for propagating joint-displacement covariances, and `adjoint_from_transform(A2B)` for transferring covariance between frames (pqac-00000011). The library renders "an equiprobable projected 6D pose hyperellipsoid at configurable kσ intervals in 3D; the example describes a 3σ hyperellipsoid, corresponding to 99.7% probability" (pqac-00000008). Importantly, the authors note that "an ellipsoid is not an appropriate representation" for all cases because rotation-translation coupling can produce banana-shaped distributions rather than ordinary ellipsoids (pqac-00000009). The library supports URDF loading through `UrdfTransformManager` and visualization through both Matplotlib and Open3D (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010). This is a peer-reviewed journal article (DOI: 10.3389/frobt.2026.1860457).

### 4.3 Path-Error Visualization

For error along a path, Issametova et al. describe evaluating the Jacobian-based reliability calculation "along θ(t), allowing reliability, variance, or error to be plotted against path position or time; trajectory planners can constrain R(θ(t)) to remain above a required threshold" (pqac-00000002). ISO 9283 also defines path accuracy and path repeatability as separate criteria measured by recording the continuous trajectory and comparing it with the commanded path (pqac-00000020).

## 5. Calibration Context: Comparative Results Across Arm Models

While this review focuses on visualization rather than calibration, several papers provide comparative positioning-accuracy results that establish the baseline these visualizations would display:

- **FANUC LR Mate 200iD/7L**: Positioning error increased from 0.018 mm (first 3 months) to 0.041 mm (last 3 months) in operational monitoring (pqac-00000039).
- **Kinova Jaco2**: Forward kinematics error of 0.82–1.48 cm, reduced to 0.29–0.50 cm with PPOE-based sensor fusion using AprilTags (pqac-00000042).
- **Franka Panda**: Mean absolute positioning error reduced from ~10 mm to 0.24–0.35 mm by MUKCa calibration; Kinova Gen3Lite improved from 5.99 mm to 2.66 mm, with remaining error attributed to "joint backlash in planetary gearboxes" (pqac-00000041). This is a preprint (DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2503.12584).
- **Generic 7-DOF arm** (Wyk et al., preprint): Bayesian remastering using a motion capture system reduced "the average robot positioning error to 0.26 mm" and the "average absolute positioning error" from over 4 mm (Vernier method) to "within 1.5 mm" with Bayesian zero-offset optimization (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034).
- **Kuka KR 16-2**: Tested per PN-EN 9283 with a Faro Vantage laser tracker (accuracy ±0.009 mm), 30 measurements per test configuration, robot warmed up with a 40-minute cycle (pqac-00000021).
- **Panasonic TM-2000**: ISO 9283 contact-measurement protocol, 1200 mm ISO cube, 5 points, 30 cycles, 50% max speed, 3 kg load, ambient 20–22°C (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026).

**No published study was found that applies error ellipsoid analysis, probabilistic workspace mapping, or ISO 9283 repeatability testing specifically to the AgileX PiPER, SO-101, or comparable sub-$200 leader-follower arms.** The closest analogue is the Kinova Gen3Lite (~$5,000), which exhibited substantial backlash-related positioning error around 2–6 mm (pqac-00000041). Low-cost arms with hobby servos and planetary gearboxes are expected to show similar or larger non-Gaussian error distributions due to backlash and compliance.

## 7. Practical Recommendations for Low-Cost Arms

For the user's AgileX PiPER and SO-101 arms with AprilTag-based measurement:

1. **Collect repeated TCP measurements** at ≥5 workspace locations following the ISO 9283 protocol (30 cycles per location, unidirectional approach). AprilTags detected by the fixed cameras provide the external position reference. Record raw 3D coordinates.

2. **Compute error ellipsoids** at each location using NumPy: subtract the mean, form the 3×3 covariance matrix, and eigendecompose. Plot 3σ ellipsoids using Matplotlib or Open3D. The eigenvalues reveal whether error is isotropic or dominated by a particular direction (e.g., gravity-axis compliance).

3. **Build a workspace map** by repeating step 1 across a grid of reachable locations. Color-code each point by its RMS repeatability and render as a 3D scatter plot. This requires no laser tracker and can be performed with the existing camera infrastructure.

4. **Use pytransform3d** for Jacobian-based propagation if a URDF model of the arm is available. The `concat_locally_uncertain_transforms` function propagates joint-angle covariances through the kinematic chain, and the projected hyperellipsoid visualization shows the resulting end-effector uncertainty (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011).

5. **Plot path error** by commanding a straight-line or circular trajectory, recording the measured TCP path at high frequency, and computing cross-track and along-track deviations as a function of path parameter. ISO 9283 path repeatability compares the envelope of deviations across multiple traversals (pqac-00000020).

## 8. Gaps in Published Evidence

- No peer-reviewed study applies error ellipsoid or probabilistic workspace mapping to the AgileX PiPER, SO-101, or comparable sub-$200 robot arms.
- No published software provides a turnkey, one-click repeatability map function; assembly from numerical primitives is required (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000019).
- The Makhambetov et al. (2026) workspace maps and error ellipsoids are simulation-only and have not been validated against physical measurements (pqac-00000019).
- Path-error visualization (error plotted continuously along a trajectory rather than at discrete points) is described mathematically but not demonstrated with dedicated software in the retrieved literature (pqac-00000002).
- The Sinha and Chakraborty (2019) robust IK work with error ellipsoids is a preprint (DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.1910.10852) and has not appeared in a peer-reviewed venue (pqac-00000030).
- The Franzese et al. (2025) MUKCa calibration study for Franka, KUKA, and Kinova is a preprint (DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2503.12584) (pqac-00000041).
