# Low-Cost vs. Industrial Robot Arms: Independently Measured Performance Data

## 1. Overview

A systematic search of peer-reviewed journals, major conference papers, and preprints from 2015–2026 was conducted to identify independently measured repeatability, accuracy, payload, speed, backlash, endurance, and failure data for low-cost arms (AgileX PiPER, SO-100/SO-101, Koch v1.1, WidowX) and industrial/research arms (UR5e, Franka Emika Panda, ABB YuMi, Kinova Gen3). The central finding is that **independently measured mechanical characterization data for most low-cost arms is sparse or absent in the published literature**, whereas industrial arms have manufacturer datasheet values that are widely cited but rarely independently verified through pose-repeatability testing. The evidence that does exist is summarized below, with clear separation of measured values from manufacturer-specified values.

## 2. Independently Measured Data for Low-Cost Arms

### 2.1 WidowX / REPLAB (~$2,000–$3,000)
Yang et al. (ICRA 2019, DOI: 10.1109/ICRA.2019.8794390) built the REPLAB benchmark around a WidowX arm. End-effector positioning performance was reported as "positions within 2 cm of the target across the 35 × 40 cm workspace floor" and "within 1 cm near the center," combining camera-calibration noise with residual control noise (yang2019replabareproduciblea pages 2-3, yang2019replabareproducible pages 2-3). No separate ISO 9283-style repeatability value was reported. A complete REPLAB cell cost "about 2000 USD" and "under $3,000" including one spare servo per joint (yang2019replabareproduciblea pages 2-3, yang2019replabareproduciblea pages 1-2). **Endurance data:** "no major breakages in more than 100,000 grasp attempts" and no servo replacements were needed (yang2019replabareproduciblea pages 2-3). **No backlash, speed, or payload measurements were published.**

### 2.2 Blue — Berkeley Open Arms (~$3,300–$5,000)
Gealy et al. (ICRA 2019, DOI: 10.1109/ICRA.2019.8794236) characterized the Blue 7-DOF quasi-direct-drive arm. Repeatability was measured using an OptiTrack motion-capture system at 100 Hz over 133 motions from a home pose to eight randomized dwell locations: "home-pose standard deviations were 0.89, 2.2, and 1.6 mm along x, y, and z" and "All return-to-home trials were within a 3.7 mm radius, with an average deviation of 2.6 mm" (gealy2019quasidirectdrivefor pages 5-6). The arm has a "2kg payload" and "nominal position-control bandwidth of 7.5Hz" (gealy2019quasidirectdrivefor pages 1-2). The prototype bill-of-materials cost was "$3,328" per arm, with a projected consumer price "below $5,000" at a run of more than 1,500 arms (gealy2019quasidirectdrivefor pages 6-8). **No backlash or endurance data were published.**

### 2.3 Forte (~$215)
Chebly et al. (arXiv **preprint**, 2025, DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2507.15693) presented Forte, a 3D-printed 6-DOF arm. Repeatability was measured with a "Westward 4KU81 dial indicator" over multiple motion cycles at motor speeds from 500 to 2,500 steps/s. The "average positional deviation across all tested speeds was 0.467 mm" with "standard deviation ranged from 0.286 mm at 500 steps/s to 0.587 mm at 2500 steps/s" (chebly2025strongaccurateand pages 6-7, chebly2025strongaccurateand pages 5-6). Payload was tested at increments up to 0.63 kg: "stable motion and positional accuracy were maintained up to 0.63 kg" with "theoretical payload of 0.96 kg" (chebly2025strongaccurateand pages 6-7). The bill-of-materials cost was "$212.40 per arm" (chebly2025strongaccurateand pages 5-6, chebly2025strongaccurateand pages 2-3). **Backlash is mentioned as a design concern but no measured value was reported.**

### 2.4 CloudGripper (~32 Cartesian arms)
Zahid & Pokorny (ICRA 2024, DOI: 10.1109/ICRA57147.2024.10611548) measured repeatability on 23 CloudGripper robots using a digital dial indicator. Each robot traversed five uniformly sampled waypoints and returned, with 10 repetitions per location. "For the XY-axis measurements, the range of deviation in position was between −0.084mm and 0.070mm and the standard deviation across all tests was calculated to be around 0.027mm." For the Z-axis (servo-driven), "the range of deviation was higher and between −0.419mm and 0.376mm with a standard deviation of approximately 0.109mm." The authors attributed Z-axis variability to "variability of the servo motors used in these robots as well as variations in the 3D-printed linear z-axis gear" (zahid2024cloudgripperanopen pages 3-5). **No arm-level price was reported.**

### 2.5 EveryDayVLA Arm (~$312)
Chopra et al. (ICRA 2026, DOI: 10.1109/ICRA57385.2026.11696905) describe a "$311.98" 6-DOF arm with "0.2 kg payload, 382 mm reach, up to 0.7 m/s end-effector speed, and 10 mm repeatability" (chopra2026everydayvlaavisionlanguageaction pages 2-3). A comparison table in the paper also lists Blue at "<$5,000" with "3.7mm" repeatability, and PAMY2 at "$15,835" (chopra2026everydayvlaavisionlanguageaction pages 2-3). The detailed measurement protocol for the 10 mm repeatability claim was not visible in the retrieved text.

### 2.6 AgileX PiPER (~$2,000), SO-100/SO-101 (~$200 in parts), Koch v1.1 (~$300)
**No published, independently measured repeatability, accuracy, backlash, payload, speed, or endurance data were found for any of these three arms.** The SO-101 is extensively used in manipulation benchmarks — Hickok (2026, preprint, DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2610.36420) reports 916 feasibility-checked benchmark episodes across four tasks (hickok2026so101benchaccessible pages 2-4), and Cowling (2026, preprint, DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2610.37170) reports 1,340 scored episodes (cowling2026from12to pages 1-3) — but these measure task-level policy success, not ISO-style mechanical properties. Cowling describes the SO-101 as costing "a few hundred dollars in parts" (cowling2026from12to pages 1-3). The AgileX PiPER appears in numerous VLA and manipulation papers but is evaluated only at the task level.

## 3. Industrial / Research Arms: Measured vs. Datasheet Values

### 3.1 Universal Robots UR5 / UR5e
**Datasheet values:** Ferraguti et al. (DATE 2019, DOI: 10.23919/DATE.2019.8714830) list the UR5 at "5 kg" payload, "±0.1 mm" repeatability, and "1000 mm/s" end-effector speed (ferraguti2019amethodologyfor pages 6-6). The UR5e datasheet specifies ±0.03 mm repeatability. The Chajon et al. (2026, DOI: 10.3390/robotics15050086) photogrammetric study tested a UR3 (not UR5e) and obtained an independent dial-indicator mean deviation of 0.0109 mm (chajon2026photogrammetriccharacterizationof pages 8-10), consistent with the manufacturer's 0.03 mm specification.

**Independently measured force-sensing:** Kirschner et al. (IROS 2021, DOI: 10.1109/IROS51168.2021.9636329) measured the UR5e's internal force repeatability error at "RPf = 0.43 N" and force-accuracy error at "Af = 5.50 N" in one reference pose (kirschner2021towardsareference pages 3-4). Ferraguti et al. separately found that the UR5's external-force estimation "RMS estimation error exceeded 13 N, with a maximum error of 55 N" (ferraguti2019amethodologyfor pages 4-5). The benchmarking protocol used 30 repetitions, an 8 N reference force from a 0.8 kg mass, two reference poses, and at least 3 seconds of acquisition per trial (kirschner2021towardsareference pages 2-3). **No independent pose-repeatability measurement for the UR5e was found in the searched literature.**

### 3.2 Franka Emika Panda
**Datasheet values:** "3 kg" payload, "±0.1 mm" repeatability, and "2000 mm/s" end-effector speed (ferraguti2019amethodologyfor pages 6-6).

**Independently measured force-sensing:** Kirschner et al. measured "RPf = 0.06 N" and "Af = 0.12 N" for the Panda's internal force sensing (kirschner2021towardsareference pages 3-4), substantially better than the UR5e. Ferraguti et al. independently found external force estimation error below "1.5 N RMS" for the Panda, compared with 13 N for the UR5 (ferraguti2019amethodologyfor pages 4-5). **No independent pose-repeatability measurement was found.**

### 3.3 Kinova Gen3
**Datasheet values only:** Raji et al. (BioMedical Engineering OnLine, 2025, DOI: 10.1186/s12938-025-01362-z) report "4.4 kg" payload, "985 mm" reach, and "±0.03 mm" repeatability in a review table (raji2025industrialgradecollaborativerobots pages 4-8). **No independent verification of these values was found.**

### 3.4 ABB YuMi (IRB 14000)
**No published independent measurement data were found** in the searched sources. YuMi appears in the rehabilitation review but without mechanical characterization (raji2025industrialgradecollaborativerobots pages 4-8).

## 4. Endurance and Failure Data

**PAMY2** (open-source tendon-driven arm, **preprint**, DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2307.02654): "operated continuously for approximately 25 days, corresponding to about 600 hours, without reported failure" during a long-term reinforcement-learning experiment. The robot "showed no significant wear or damage" (guist2023arobustopensource pages 7-8, guist2023arobustopensource pages 2-3). Material cost: approximately €14,185 (guist2023arobustopensource pages 5-6).

**WidowX/REPLAB:** Over 100,000 grasp attempts with zero servo replacements (yang2019replabareproduciblea pages 2-3).

**For AgileX PiPER, SO-100/SO-101, Koch v1.1, UR5e, Franka Panda, Kinova Gen3, and ABB YuMi: no published endurance or systematic failure-rate data were found** in the peer-reviewed or preprint literature searched.

## 5. Backlash

**No independently measured backlash values were found for any of the specific arms queried** (AgileX PiPER, SO-100/SO-101, Koch v1.1, WidowX, UR5e, Franka Panda, Kinova Gen3, ABB YuMi). The Forte paper mentions backlash as a design concern but provides no measured value (chebly2025strongaccurateand pages 2-3). The CloudGripper paper attributes Z-axis variability partly to servo-motor inconsistency but does not quantify backlash separately (zahid2024cloudgripperanopen pages 3-5). An open-source test stand for backlash measurement in UART servos (Kotov 2026, DOI: 10.1016/j.ohx.2026.e00767) was identified but could not be retrieved in full text.

## 8. Key Gaps and Recommendations

**Where no published evidence exists:**

- **AgileX PiPER:** No published ISO 9283-style repeatability, accuracy, backlash, payload capacity, maximum speed, or endurance test. The arm appears in dozens of VLA and embodied AI papers but exclusively at the task-success level.
- **SO-100/SO-101:** No published mechanical characterization despite extensive use in LeRobot and VLA benchmarks. Task-level success is well documented (916+ episodes in SO-101 Bench, 1,340 episodes in Cowling 2026).
- **Koch v1.1:** No published mechanical characterization of any kind.
- **Backlash:** No arm in either the low-cost or industrial category has a published, independently measured backlash value in the retrieved literature.
- **Systematic endurance/failure rates:** No mean-time-between-failure or systematic reliability study was found for any of the arms of interest.

**Recommendation for self-measurement:** The ISO 9283 protocol (30 repetitions per pose at 5 workspace positions, mean deviation + 3σ metric) is the most standardized and repeatable approach. Chajón et al. (2026) demonstrated that a Raspberry Pi 4 camera with ArUco markers and OpenCV can achieve sub-millimeter external pose measurement at low cost (chajon2026photogrammetriccharacterizationof pages 8-10), providing an accessible measurement chain for characterizing your PiPER and SO-101 arms. The REPLAB grasping protocol (yang2019replabareproduciblea pages 3-4) provides a directly applicable task-level benchmark for your parallel-gripper setup.
