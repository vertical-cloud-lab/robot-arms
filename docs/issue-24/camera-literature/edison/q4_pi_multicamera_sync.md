## Query

Has anyone built a multi-camera 3D tracking, motion-capture or photogrammetry system from Raspberry Pi cameras in which either several cameras share one Raspberry Pi through CSI ribbon cables of about 0.5 m or longer, or each camera has its own Raspberry Pi synchronized over the network (NTP, PTP or a hardware trigger), and what frame-synchronization accuracy, cable-length limits or signal-integrity problems were reported?

## Answer

Question: Has anyone built a multi-camera 3D tracking, motion-capture or photogrammetry system from Raspberry Pi cameras in which either several cameras share one Raspberry Pi through CSI ribbon cables of about 0.5 m or longer, or each camera has its own Raspberry Pi synchronized over the network (NTP, PTP or a hardware trigger), and what frame-synchronization accuracy, cable-length limits or signal-integrity problems were reported?

Yes. ## Short answer

**Yes for distributed Raspberry Pi camera arrays; not verified for the specified long-ribbon, shared-Pi arrangement.** A published build used Raspberry Pi cameras on networked Pis to generate dynamic 3D point clouds. A separate eight-Pi tracking system measured exceptionally close frame alignment, but tracked animals in **2D**, not 3D. In the sources examined, I could not verify a built 3D-tracking, motion-capture, or photogrammetry system connecting several cameras to *one* Pi with **bare CSI ribbon cables ≥0.5 m**. Nor did I find a measured maximum usable bare-ribbon length or cable-length-dependent image-error rate for such a system. (ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 33-39, ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 46-53, saxena2018inexpensivescalablecamera pages 5-8, saxena2018inexpensivescalablecamera pages 15-18)

| **Ren, *Dynamic Thermal 3D Point Cloud Generation Using Structure from Motion* (2024)** | Built a 14-Pi cluster—one server and 13 clients—and an array of **12 Raspberry Pi HQ RGB cameras plus one FLIR Boson thermal camera**. It reports reconstructed dynamic thermal 3D point clouds, including a hand and sand. | A local server supplied **NTP over wired Ethernet**; capture commands could be broadcast with parallel SSH. For the 13 listed wired clients, measured **NTP clock offsets** ranged from **−0.155 to +0.768 ms**, jitter from **0.016 to 0.941 ms**, and network delays from **0.061 to 0.181 ms**. Three Wi-Fi test Pis instead showed offsets of **−17.164 to −2.070 ms**, jitter of **1.869–15.420 ms**, and delays of **23.029–35.787 ms**. These are *clock/network statistics, not measured differences between camera exposure times*. The author considered PTP unavailable on the Pi 4B configuration used; no camera hardware trigger or exposure-skew test is reported. | Each Pi-to-switch **Cat 6e Ethernet cable was 1.5 m**. That is **not** a CSI ribbon length; the camera ribbons and their signal integrity were not characterized. The wired-versus-Wi-Fi problems reported were wireless congestion, interference, obstruction and packet loss—not CSI image corruption. (ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 33-39, ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 39-46, ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 46-53, ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 27-33) |
| **Saxena, Barde and Deshmukh, *Journal of Neurophysiology* (2018)** | Eight Raspberry Pi 2/Camera Module v1 units tracked rats across a 5.5 × 3 m arena. **Relevant engineering precedent, but 2D position tracking rather than 3D reconstruction.** | Network connections provided remote control, **not NTP camera synchronization**. An Arduino delivered common TTL pulses whose timestamps aligned Pi camera-frame timestamps to a data-acquisition clock; the paper does **not** establish that the TTL directly triggered all camera shutters. Camera starts were within **0.5 ms** in multiple sessions; subsequent frame times stayed within **0.5 ms**, with **<0.05 ms** separation in one illustrated four-hour recording. Reported clock drift relative to acquisition was **<13 µs** over up to four hours. At 30 Hz there were no dropped frames in four hours; at 50 and 60 Hz the reported drop rates were **0.22%** and **0.31%**, respectively. Inter-frame-interval jitter and inter-camera frame offset are distinct measurements. | One camera was attached to each Pi, but CSI cable length and cable-related errors were **not reported**. Figure 5a presents the across-camera frame-time comparison. Its **0.64-mm** and **0.73-mm** results are *spatial registration errors*, not synchronization accuracies. | The inspected report does not specify ≥0.5-m CSI ribbons or a cable signal-integrity test. **What the ribbon-cable evidence actually says.** A documented conventional Pi-camera connection uses a **15-cm CSI ribbon**. A Pi-based single-camera 3D-print measurement project explicitly says ordinary CSI ribbons are only several centimetres long and instead uses **CSI-to-HDMI adapter boards and an HDMI cable** to place its camera farther away; it specifies neither that cable’s length nor a maximum reliable bare-CSI length. Another build lengthened ribbons for two cameras on an Arducam multiplexer but was a balloon-video system, **not 3D reconstruction**; its reported pixelation and lag arose from **video-transmission bandwidth**, not a demonstrated CSI electrical failure. None of those examples validates a ≥0.5-m bare ribbon carrying several cameras to one Pi. (parker2026establishingaformalised pages 93-98, zbroja2026laserbasedvolumemeasurement pages 2-3, dickinson2024engineeringexperiencesand pages 7-9, dickinson2024engineeringexperiencesand pages 9-11)

**Important exclusions:** A photogrammetric underwater three-camera rig used a Pi Zero W to issue hardware triggers, but its **Basler cameras connected over Ethernet to a laptop**, not through Pi CSI ports. A 300-camera volumetric studio used a Pi as an **NTP server** while **Jetson** boards hosted the cameras; its measured **16.67-ms** camera-synchronization bound must not be presented as a Pi-camera result. A 100-Pi light-field array used one CSI camera per Pi but did not report NTP/PTP/trigger accuracy or CSI cable lengths, and its reported demonstration was computational refocusing rather than verified 3D motion capture. (rofallski2020measuringartificialreefs pages 2-3, rofallski2020measuringartificialreefs pages 3-4, heagerty2024holocameraadvancedvolumetric pages 3-4, heagerty2024holocameraadvancedvolumetric pages 6-7, heagerty2024holocameraadvancedvolumetric pages 7-9, pschipsch2026nearfarwherever pages 3-4, pschipsch2026nearfarwherever pages 8-10)

**Interpretation:** Ren establishes that a networked, one-Pi-per-view approach has produced 3D photogrammetric output; Saxena establishes a separate, well-measured hardware-*timestamp*-alignment approach. Ren’s sub-millisecond **NTP offsets do not prove sub-millisecond simultaneous exposures**. Conversely, the available literature does not establish that 0.5-m bare CSI ribbons are either reliable or impossible: the specific requested multi-camera 3D implementation and its cable-length/signal-integrity measurements remain unverified. (ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 33-39, ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 39-46, ren2024dynamicthermal3d pages 46-53, saxena2018inexpensivescalablecamera pages 5-8, saxena2018inexpensivescalablecamera pages 15-18, zbroja2026laserbasedvolumemeasurement pages 2-3)
