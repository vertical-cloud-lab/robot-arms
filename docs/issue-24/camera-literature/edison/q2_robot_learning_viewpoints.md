# Fixed-camera viewpoints for a tabletop PiPER

**Bottom line.** An overhead camera plus two oblique cameras is a defensible design for *workspace monitoring and independent fingertip triangulation*. It is **not** an arrangement that the policy-learning literature has established as uniquely optimal. Published systems commonly combine a global external view with a front or low-angle view; many also use wrist views. Their papers generally do **not** report reproducible camera heights, distances, or pitch angles. Place the third camera to supply *different visibility and triangulation geometry*, rather than simply another similar-looking image. (zhao2023learningfinegrainedbimanual pages 2-3, chuang2025activevisionmight pages 3-4, khazatsky2024droidalargescale pages 3-4, chuang2025activevisionmight pages 5-6)

## Quantitative evidence: view *information* matters more than a universal view ranking

**Camera count and occlusion.** AV-ALOHA trained ACT policies with seven combinations of fixed scene (“Static”), wrist, and *actively repositioned* (“AV”) observations. For the relatively visible simulated **slot-insertion** task, full-task success was **66% Static**, **78% Static + Wrist**, but **36% AV + Static + Wrist**. For **hooking a package**, it was **44% Static**, **30% Static + Wrist**, and **24% with all three sources**. Conversely, for visually challenging **threading**, it was **30% Static**, **52% AV**, and **52% AV + Wrist**; for the real **hidden-pick** task, **40% Static** versus **60% AV + Wrist**, but **15% with all three**. Thus, *more camera inputs are not automatically better*, and views that reveal an otherwise hidden target can be disproportionately useful. These comparisons involve moving cameras and an active-camera control problem—not an ablation of two versus three **fixed** exterior cameras. Simulation results selected the best among 12 checkpoints and used 50 rollouts per checkpoint; real-task configurations used 20 rollouts. (chuang2025activevisionmight pages 5-6, chuang2025activevisionmight pages 4-5, chuang2025activevisionmight pages 3-4)

In a separate multi-view world-model study, adding a **left-shoulder view** to **front + wrist** did **not substantially improve** performance on three evaluated tasks. That is useful evidence of diminishing returns, but it does not test whether adding a *fixed low side view to a top-down-only PiPER rig* helps. (seo2023multiviewmaskedworld pages 19-20)

**Front versus oblique/side.** A 2026 StereoPolicy simulation study evaluated **ten external camera angles** on ToolHang. Relative to a monocular policy, stereo fusion’s reported gains were **18 percentage points at frontview** and **14 points at elevated frontview**; side views benefited less, with the authors attributing this to poorer overlap and self-occlusion. This compares *stereo and monocular methods at each view*, **not** absolute success for overhead versus front versus side; it does not establish that a front camera is always best. On ToolHang with 200 demonstrations, removing wrist observations reduced the stereo policy from **96% to 94%**, while an RGB comparison fell **90% to 86%**. Those figures likewise do not imply that two widely separated Pi camera units will behave like a closely spaced, explicitly fused stereo pair. (han2026stereopolicyimprovingrobotic pages 7-9, han2026stereopolicyimprovingrobotic pages 22-24)

**Shifts after training.** Camera placement is a genuine deployment variable. In a real *cup-on-saucer* test, a policy trained on one third-person pose plus a wrist camera achieved **4/40 full-task successes** aggregated over four changed exterior viewpoints. Novel-view augmentation improved aggregate success to **14/50**, or **20/50** when its view generator was additionally adapted on DROID data. These denominators and viewpoint sets are **not identical**—the unaugmented policy’s fifth-camera result was omitted—so interpret the comparison as evidence of brittleness and useful augmentation, not a clean percentage-point estimate for a specified camera displacement. (tian2024viewinvariantpolicylearning pages 7-8)

In the Colosseum simulated perturbation protocol, front and shoulder cameras’ positions can each change by up to **0.1 m per coordinate** and Euler angles by up to **0.05 rad per coordinate** (about **2.9°**); its real extension also changes and recalibrates the front-camera position. These are *test perturbation ranges*, **not recommended mounting tolerances**. In a separate fixed-camera training study, a conventional simulated behavior-cloning policy scored **0%** on easy and hard camera shifts in three evaluated tasks despite canonical successes of **51.7–63.3%**; a different object-centric 3D method retained task-dependent nonzero success. Because the methods and representations differ, this tests robustness, not an overhead-versus-side ranking. (pumacay2024thecolosseuma pages 4-5, pumacay2024thecolosseuma pages 5-7, zhu2310learninggeneralizablemanipulation pages 6-7)

Evidence specifically for **over-the-shoulder** views is also conditional. A 2026 study deliberately sampled two over-the-shoulder cameras and a wrist view, randomizing calibrated RGB-D camera poses during training. On its Cabinet simulation, its geometry-grounded policy attained **64.4 normalized opening** with randomized cameras versus **39.5** for a camera-conditioned comparison; **normalized opening is not a task-success percentage**. It requires calibrated extrinsics and depth and therefore does not directly predict performance from three uncalibrated RGB-only cameras. (nakura2026anempiricalstudy pages 4-5, nakura2026anempiricalstudy pages 5-6)

## Implications for the proposed geometry

**Overhead at 1.2 m.** Using the stated **102° × 67°** field of view and an ideal rectilinear, level camera, table-plane coverage centered on its optical axis is approximately **2 × 1.2 tan(51°) = 2.96 m** horizontally and **2 × 1.2 tan(33.5°) = 1.59 m** vertically. This nominally exceeds the **1.29 × 1.36 m** tabletop when the **67° axis follows the 1.36 m dimension**, with only about **0.12 m margin at each end** of that dimension if centered. To fit the full 1.36 m in the narrow axis, the ideal minimum height is about **1.03 m**; the 1.2 m proposal therefore has limited tolerance for decentering, tall objects, an elevated gripper, or a narrower effective field after distortion correction. These are *geometric calculations from the specifications in your question*, not published policy results. An overhead image gives excellent table-plane context but cannot, on its own, resolve fingertip height from a single image.

**End-facing camera at 0.42 m height, 0.8 m from the base.** Its nominal optical-axis drop over **0.8 m** at **19°** is only **0.28 m**. If it is aimed toward a table-plane point near the base, the required declination is approximately **arctan(0.42/0.8) = 27.7°**, not 19°. More importantly, at **19°** the *bottom* vertical field boundary points **52.5° downward**. At the base’s range, that boundary intersects the tabletop only **0.33 m horizontally from the camera**; whether the important near edge or floor is visible depends on where the camera actually sits relative to the tabletop. Conversely, the top boundary points **14.5° upward**, providing room to see elevated gripper positions. These angles do **not** by themselves determine usable coverage: roll orientation, which sensor dimension is horizontal, image distortion, clipping by the table edge, and the base’s offset from that edge must be checked with sample frames. Aim at the **center of the fingertip’s *3D operating volume***, not simply at the arm base. Calculations here use your stated pose and FOV, not a reported mounting geometry from a dataset.

**Third, low room-side edge camera.** Keep it sufficiently **oblique and separated in azimuth** from the end-facing view that a fingertip obscured by the gripper, arm, or an object in one side image remains visible in another. A low view contributes evidence of clearance, contact height, and the near side of objects that top-down imagery may conceal; an *extremely* low grazing angle instead risks table-edge occlusion and overlapping fingertips. Position and angle should be chosen by measuring visibility of **both fingertips** across sampled joint configurations, rather than by copying a platform label such as “sideview.” High/low viewpoint complementarity is supported by ALOHA 2’s hardware choice; the need to inspect occlusion is supported by the controlled view experiments. (chuang2025activevisionmight pages 3-4, chuang2025activevisionmight pages 5-6, han2026stereopolicyimprovingrobotic pages 7-9)

**For the few-millimetre drift objective, camera count alone is insufficient.** A back-of-envelope ideal overhead image scale is roughly **2.96 m/4608 ≈ 0.64 mm per pixel** along the wide axis and **1.59 m/2592 ≈ 0.61 mm per pixel** along the narrow axis *at table height*. Those are projected sampling pitches, **not 3D accuracy**: fingertip localization, lens distortion, limited stereo intersection angles, exposure/motion blur, synchronization, and robot-to-camera calibration introduce further error. Calibrate intrinsics and distortion for the actual focus setting, estimate each fixed camera’s extrinsics in one **table/robot-base frame**, and verify *held-out 3D fingertip errors* throughout the reachable volume against a separately measured target. DROID’s repeated extrinsic calibration and RH20T’s multiview camera-to-robot calibration illustrate the distinction between recording extra images and obtaining comparable 3D measurements. Do not fit camera extrinsics to the same potentially drifting joint-angle kinematics you intend to audit without an independent check: doing so can absorb the drift into the calibration. (khazatsky2024droidalargescale pages 3-4, fang2024rh20tacomprehensive pages 4-5)

**Recommendation.** Install the overhead camera for global clearance and two *nonredundant* oblique cameras for fingertip visibility and depth geometry; the proposed end-facing plus room-side arrangement is a reasonable **starting configuration**, not a literature-validated optimum. First map each camera’s actual field of view and fingertip occlusion over the robot’s working poses; next calibrate and quantify 3D reconstruction error before claiming detection of a few millimetres of kinematic drift. For learned policies, retain consistent camera poses between demonstrations and deployment, and compare overhead-only, overhead + best side, and all three **on your own tasks**. Existing ablations demonstrate that task-dependent visibility helps, added views sometimes add nothing or hurt, and untrained camera shifts can be severe; they do not support a universal prescription of exactly three fixed views. (chuang2025activevisionmight pages 5-6, seo2023multiviewmaskedworld pages 19-20, tian2024viewinvariantpolicylearning pages 7-8, zhu2310learninggeneralizablemanipulation pages 6-7)
