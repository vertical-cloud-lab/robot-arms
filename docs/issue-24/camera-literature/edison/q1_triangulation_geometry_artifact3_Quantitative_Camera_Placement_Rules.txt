| Preferred ray-intersection angle | For isolated-point triangulation with comparable angular noise, aim near a right-angle intersection; **60–120°** is a useful practical band. Angle definitions vary across papers, so compare the actual angle between rays at the target. | Ideal ≈ **90°**; practical ≈ **60–120°** | Simulations found minimum height error near 90° and minimum overall position error near 60°; strongly convergent networks produced homogeneous 3D precision. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000028) |
| Rectified-stereo depth uncertainty | `Z = f_px B / d`, hence `σ_Z ≈ Z² σ_d / (f_px B)`. Equivalently, `σ_Z ≈ Z² p σ_d / (f_phys B)`, where `p` is pixel pitch and `σ_d` is in pixels. | Error grows as **distance²** and falls linearly with **baseline** and **focal length in pixels**. | (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) |
| Fraser network comparison | Symmetric convergent imaging makes coordinate precision more isotropic than normal or near-parallel imaging; mixed normal-plus-convergent geometry is also strong. | Mean accuracy factor `q`: **0.6** at 120° convergence versus **0.7–1.0** for the two normal networks; 120° gave `σ_XY = σ_Z = 0.07 mm` in the simulation. | (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000028) |
| Moderate oblique imagery | Supplementing nadir coverage with oblique images increases parallax, reduces systematic deformation, and fills visibility gaps. | Oblique tilt **20–35° from nadir**; nearly **50%** improvement in accuracy and precision over nadir-only blocks in the studied high-relief scene. | (pqac-00000018) |
| Occlusion-resistant dense capture | For full-body surface reconstruction, distribute cameras non-coplanarly around the subject; neighboring views should retain enough appearance similarity for matching. These counts are not a minimum for fingertip triangulation. | Neighboring angular baseline ≈ **25°**; visibility coverage stabilized near **16 cameras** in the reported studio experiment. | (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000036) |
| Li et al. convergence optimum | Error minima occurred over a range rather than at one universal angle; well-distributed tie points changed the optimum. | With 6 landmarks, optimum ≈ **99–100°**; with 9–12, approximately **100–108°** in two tested camera systems; operational band cited as **70–90°**. | (pqac-00000042) |
| Benefit of additional cameras | Once geometry is strong, precision improves approximately with the square root of observation count: `σ_XYZ ∝ 1 / √m`. Geometry matters more than merely adding similar views. | Moving from **2 to 3** cameras gives a substantial gain; **3 to 4** yields about **20%** lower standard error under the approximation. | (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) |
| Excessively wide view differences | Larger intersection angles improve geometry only until correspondence and target-localization errors rise from foreshortening and appearance distortion. Thus, the geometric 90° preference is not an instruction to maximize angle indefinitely. | Di et al.: height-error curve minimum near **90°**, then increases; Olague–Mohr’s target-localization model rose sharply as incidence approached **80°**. | (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000046) |
