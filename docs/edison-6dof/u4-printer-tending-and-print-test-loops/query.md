Context. We run a small cloud lab at BYU (the Vertical Cloud Lab) with an AgileX PiPER 6-DOF arm (about $2.5k, 1.5 kg payload, roughly 15 N of gripping force, wrist joint J5 limited to plus or minus 70 degrees, so no straight-down grasps near the table), SO-101 leader/follower arms, Raspberry Pi 5 wrist and fixed cameras, AprilTags, and Bambu Lab printers (two A1 mini bed-slingers with 180 mm magnetic textured-PEI plates, and an H2D). Prints are sent remotely by an AI agent after a human approval, and the agent will not send a print until a camera frame shows the plate clear; today a person removes every part. We want the arm to clear plates (first for a batch of small toys on the A1 minis in December), and later to move printed specimens into mechanical tests inside closed design-print-test loops: 3D-printed tensegrity structures under drop or compression tests, and printed fixtures for our alloy and powder workflows. A previous literature answer claimed that no publication shows an arm unloading 3D printers inside a closed experimental loop; we believe Boston University's BEAR and MAMA BEAR systems (Gongora et al. 2020, Science Advances; Snapp et al. 2024, Nature Communications) contradict this, and we also know of SmartLab (2025), TeleopLab, and commercial plate-swapping systems such as Prusa's automatic sheet changer.

Question.
(1) Which academic and industrial systems use a robot arm (or other mechanism) to remove parts from FFF/FDM printers or swap build plates? For each: the removal method (flexing a magnetic spring-steel sheet, scraping or pushing, swapping the whole plate, tilting, ejection by the print head), the printers and arms used (especially any low-cost arms), measured success rates with trial counts, cycle times, failure modes (parts stuck when warm, small or tall parts tipping, adhesion changes with plate wear), and the bed temperature or cool-down conditions that make release reliable.
(2) Closed-loop design-print-test systems with robotic specimen handling (BEAR and MAMA BEAR, SmartLab, and any others): what the arm does, how specimens reach testing machines (universal testers, compression, impact or drop rigs), throughput, uptime, human interventions or failure rates reported, and the limitations and future work their authors state.
(3) Camera-based print monitoring and verification relevant to a robot cell: detecting print failures (detachment, spaghetti, warping, layer shift) with low-cost cameras and reported accuracy; checking that the plate is clear before the next print; and automatic measurement of printed specimens (dimensions, mass, defects) before testing.
(4) Print farms with robotic tending: published throughput, uptime, human hours per print and cost per part, versus manual farms.
(5) Remote or cloud users who submit design-print-test jobs (for example BEAR's external-user pilots or teleoperated test labs): measured outcomes such as completion rate, turnaround and interventions.
(6) Given our arm's limits, which removal methods are feasible for small parts on a 180 mm bed-slinger, what forces are involved (adhesion force of PLA or PETG on textured PEI, sheet-flex force), and what is known about grasping irregular printed parts with a parallel gripper?

Prefer peer-reviewed sources from 2015 to 2026, and flag preprints, theses, and commercial or trade sources. For each claim give the DOI or URL and quote the sentence that supports it. Say explicitly where no published evidence exists.
