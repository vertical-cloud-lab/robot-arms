Context. We run a small cloud lab at BYU (the Vertical Cloud Lab) with an AgileX PiPER 6-DOF arm and Raspberry Pi 5 computers carrying Pi HQ (IMX477) and Camera Module 3 cameras, both fixed around the cell and on the wrist. Today we localize fixtures with AprilTags (25 mm tags read reliably at about 1 m; 10 mm tags did not read at all), no camera is calibrated to the arm yet, and the cell is livestreamed at 720p and 5 frames per second with on-device face blurring. We are starting a ledger of every handling step and every human intervention (for 3D-printer plate clearing, loading vials into a gantry liquid handler, and moving SEM stubs and sealed powder jars), and we want the cameras to do more than localize: verify outcomes (plate clear, vial seated, stub inserted, jar on the balance, powder spilled), detect human interventions and hands entering the cell automatically so the ledger does not depend on people remembering to log, and notice when a camera's calibration has drifted. Our 3D-printing workflow already refuses to start a print until a camera frame shows the plate clear, but nobody has measured how often such a check is wrong. We know of LIRA (Zhou et al. 2025; a fine-tuned vision-language model on an edge box caught 195 of 200 hand-placed errors), Gemini Robotics ER 2 (reported 82.4% video success detection), the Robometer and TOPReward success scorers in LeRobot, multi-tag fiducials such as quARtet, and targetless wrist-plus-fixed camera calibration (AnchorHEC).

Question.
(1) What accuracy have vision and vision-language success or failure detectors achieved on real robot manipulation outcomes, especially laboratory tasks? Report precision, recall and false-success rates with trial counts, how results change with viewpoint, resolution, frame rate and lighting, and which models run on edge hardware such as a Raspberry Pi 5 with or without an accelerator, or a Jetson.
(2) Automatic detection of human interventions and human presence in robot workcells from ordinary cameras (hand or body detection, pose estimation, activity recognition): measured accuracy and latency, use for logging versus for safety, and the status of camera-based safety functions under ISO 13849, IEC 61496 and ISO 10218:2025.
(3) Camera verification of placement and insertion (seated or not, offset estimates) for labware: measured accuracy and the smallest offsets detected, for example in LIRA, Fontenot et al. 2026, or rack-insertion work.
(4) Calibration maintenance: methods to detect extrinsic drift of fixed and wrist cameras during long campaigns (self-checks against fixture tags, targetless methods), how often recalibration was needed, and any results for low-cost cameras such as the IMX477.
(5) How should the false-clear or false-success rate of a vision check that gates the next action (such as starting a print) be measured and bounded, and what rates have been reported for such gates?
(6) Privacy and governance for continuously recorded or livestreamed laboratory video (face blurring, retention), and whether anonymization degrades detection.

Prefer peer-reviewed sources from 2015 to 2026, and flag preprints. For each claim give the DOI or arXiv ID and quote the sentence that supports it. Say explicitly where no published evidence exists.
