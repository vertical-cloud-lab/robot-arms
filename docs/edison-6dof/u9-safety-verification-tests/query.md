Context. We run a small university cloud lab at BYU (the Vertical Cloud Lab) with a low-cost AgileX PiPER 6-DOF arm (about $2.5k, 1.5 kg payload, no joint brakes, no certified safety functions; it sags when power is removed), driven over CAN from a Raspberry Pi, with its supply switched by an IoT relay and a magnetic-switch E-stop on the supply. Our current state: the E-stop has never been tested; rebooting the controlling Pi cuts the arm's power mid-move and the arm drops; a hazard analysis and SOP are drafted but not reviewed; unattended runs are not allowed yet; the arm shares an enclosure with Raspberry Pi cameras and will tend consumer 3D printers and move vials, SEM stubs and sealed powder jars near people. An earlier literature answer already covered safety architecture (hardwired dual-channel E-stops that bypass software, safe states versus de-energizing, retry policies, no unattended combustible metal powder). We now need the verification side: how to test and document that what we built actually works, at university-lab cost.

Question.
(1) Test procedures and acceptance criteria for emergency-stop and protective-stop functions on small robot arms: what IEC 60204-1 (stop categories 0, 1, 2), ISO 13850, ISO 13849-2 (validation) and ISO 10218-1/-2:2025 require or recommend; how stopping time and stopping distance are measured (instruments, number of repetitions, worst-case pose and payload); and published examples of such tests on cobots or low-cost arms.
(2) Arms without brakes: published measurements or analyses of what happens on power loss (drop distance and speed under gravity, by pose and payload), and mitigations used on low-cost or hobby arms (mechanical brakes, back-drive resistance, controlled-stop before de-energizing, rest poses, UPS-backed controllers, supported park positions).
(3) Collision testing with inexpensive instruments: measuring transient and quasi-static contact force and pressure against ISO/TS 15066 body-region limits using force gauges, load cells, pressure-sensitive film or low-cost biofidelic pads, with reported accuracy versus commercial collision-measurement devices; and measured collision forces for low-cost arms (PiPER, SO-100/SO-101, myCobot, xArm Lite, UFactory or similar) if any exist.
(4) Risk assessment and documentation for robots in academic research labs: what university EHS programs (published policies, guidance documents or papers) require before a robot cell runs, including attended versus unattended operation, training, signage, interlocks, and periodic re-verification; and any incident reports involving low-cost or educational robot arms.
(5) A minimal, defensible verification plan for a cell like ours: which tests to run first, with how many repetitions, and what records to keep.

Prefer peer-reviewed sources and published standards or official guidance from 2010 to 2026; flag preprints, theses and vendor documents. For each claim give the DOI, standard clause or URL and quote the supporting sentence. Say explicitly where no published evidence exists.
