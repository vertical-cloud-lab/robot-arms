Context. We run a small cloud lab at BYU (the Vertical Cloud Lab) with an AgileX PiPER 6-DOF arm (about $2.5k, 1.5 kg payload, parallel gripper, about 15 N available at the flange), SO-101 leader/follower arms for teleoperation and imitation learning, Raspberry Pi 5 wrist and fixed cameras, AprilTags and 3D-printed fixtures. Our alloy program (FLAIME) is an AI-guided closed loop for aluminium alloys made by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF): CALPHAD screening and cost-aware Bayesian optimization choose compositions; charges of 250 to 300 g (machined 6063 cups with press-fit plugs, or bar slugs and master-alloy pieces) are atomized in an AMAZEMET rePOWDER ultrasonic atomizer; then the powder is collected, weighed in six size fractions, sieved, split, jarred and labelled, sampled and sealed for oxygen analysis, tested for flowability, and mounted on SEM pin stubs (seven or eight per run) for morphology and EDS; printed specimens (single tracks, density cubes, ASTM E8 sub-size tensile bars) go to Archimedes density, Vickers hardness, metallography and tensile testing. Our own schedule estimates 10 to 15 person-hours of hands-on work per atomizer run, mostly after the run, and none of it is timed yet. Aluminium powder is a combustible metal (NFPA 484, now NFPA 660); operators wear full-face respirators for open-powder work; and the arm will stay out of the atomizer chamber. Peer efforts we know of include CMU's Materials Innovation Cloud Lab (also a rePOWDER), Texas A&M's metals self-driving lab, robotic metallography efforts, and printer-based SEM powder preparation (Milsted et al. 2025, EMSBot).

Question. What is the state of the art (2015 to 2026) in automating the hands-on steps of powder-metallurgy and additive-manufacturing alloy-development loops, and which steps have measured results?
(1) Post-atomization powder workup: powder collection, sieving and classification (loading sieve shakers), riffling or splitting, mass balance, jarring and labelling, and sealing samples for O/N/H analysis. Which have been automated with arms or other equipment, with what throughput, success rate, recovery and cross-contamination control between alloys?
(2) Powder characterization sample preparation: dispersing powder onto SEM stubs, automated SEM imaging and particle-shape analysis, sample introduction for laser-diffraction particle sizing, and automated flowability, apparent and tap density measurements.
(3) Solid-specimen steps: removing parts from LPBF build plates, sectioning, mounting, grinding and polishing (robotic metallography), automated Archimedes density, automated Vickers or micro-hardness, and robotic loading of tensile specimens. Give reported success rates, cycle times and failure modes.
(4) Charge preparation: robotic weighing and kitting of alloy charges from pieces or powders, and crucible loading.
(5) Safety: what NFPA 484/660, ATEX or published guidance requires of robots and electronics that handle open or closed combustible metal powders (ignition sources, grounding, dust-tight enclosures, inerting), any incident reports, and how autonomous metal-powder labs (including A-Lab follow-ups under inert atmosphere) handle this.
(6) Published time-per-sample or person-hour breakdowns for powder-metallurgy or AM alloy development that show where human hands dominate, to check our estimate that post-run workup is the bottleneck.
(7) For each step, judge whether it is realistically within reach of a 1.5 kg payload, low-force arm with a parallel gripper and Raspberry Pi cameras, or needs industrial equipment (for example press forces above a tonne).

Prefer peer-reviewed sources from 2015 to 2026, and flag preprints and conference abstracts. For each claim give the DOI and quote the sentence that supports it. Say explicitly where no published evidence exists.
