# Study design table

One row per hypothesis, in the columns Peer Community In Registered Reports (PCI RR) asks Stage 1
authors to fill. PCI RR's site blocks automated reading, so I couldn't check its current template:
compare the columns with it before a Stage 1. The table is useful for the OSF registration too, as
a one-page summary. Details are in [`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md) §2, §8 and §14; powers are from
[`analysis/out/power.md`](analysis/out/power.md).

| Question | Hypothesis | Sampling plan | Analysis plan | Rationale for the test's sensitivity | Interpretation given different outcomes | Theory that could be shown wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does a 3 mm lead-in dock make open-loop carrier loading reliable enough for unattended runs? | **H1a**: *p*(carrier, OL) ≥ 99 % | E1: 300 loads (or 628, D5), randomized sub-blocks with the other three cells | Exact one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson bounds | 99 % per transfer makes a 50-transfer run more likely than not to finish (61 %). At *n* = 300, power is 55 % at a true 99.8 % and 74 % at 99.9 %; at *n* = 628, 87 % and 97 % | Lower bound ≥ 0.99: the dock alone is enough for this task. Upper bound < 0.99: it is not, and correction or a better fixture is needed even for carriers. Otherwise inconclusive, and the cell is not extended | That passive mechanical capture is enough for reliable transfers with a low-cost arm |
| Is a tight pocket the bottleneck for single vials? | **H1b**: *p*(vial, OL) < 99 % into ≤ 1 mm clearance | E1: 300 loads | Exact one-sided 95 % upper bound | 80 % power if the true rate is 97 %, 99 % at 95 % | Supported: tight single-vial pockets need help (correction, chamfers). Refuted: the arm's error is already inside ±0.5 mm | That the arm's error budget (±0.64 mm RSS) exceeds a tight pocket's capture |
| Does the dock capture more error than the arm makes? | **H2**: ED90 > 1.15 mm on x and y (carrier, OL) | E2: 21 offset levels × 20 loads; per axis 20 at 0 and 40 at each of 1–4 mm | Firth logistic in \|offset\|; with a significantly negative slope, the Fieller one-sided 95 % lower bound of ED90 (monotone pooled bound if censored); refuted per axis at 99 % | Supported 97–100 % of the time if the true ED90 ≥ 2 mm, ≤ 0.2 % at 1.15 mm; refuted 1.0–5.1 % of the time at exactly 1.15 mm, 79–100 % at 0.8 mm | Supported: the dock's margin exceeds the worst-case error, so failures that remain are not geometric. Refuted: the lead-in is too small for this arm | The design error budget (byu-vcl #266) and the lead-in's capture range |
| Does wrist-camera correction raise single-vial success? | **H3a**: *p*(vial, AT) > *p*(vial, OL) | E1: 300 + 300 loads, conditions randomized within sub-blocks | One-sided Boschloo exact test | 90 % power for 95 % → 99 %, 72 % for 97 % → 99.5 %, 48 % for 97 % → 99 % | Supported: correction closes part of the gap (*G* reported). Contrary: correction makes single vials worse. Not supported: no gain detectable at this *n*. At 300 per arm the test reliably detects 95 % → 99 % but not 97 % → 99 %, so a null rules out only large gains | That fixture-pose error, not grasp error, dominates single-vial failures |
| Does correction widen the capture envelope? | **H3b**: ED90_AT > ED90_OL on x and y (vials) | E2: as H2, both conditions paired at every level | Delta-method one-sided 95 % lower bound of the capture-radius difference (Fieller if AT is censored) | 100 % power for 1.0 vs 0.3 mm; 2.5 % for 0.6 vs 0.3 mm (the 1 mm spacing can't resolve it); 0–1.4 % false support, even when the slopes differ | Supported: correction tolerates displaced fixtures. Not supported: it adds little beyond the pocket | That measuring the fixture's pose recovers its displacement |
| What does correction cost? | **H3c**: extra time ≤ D8 s per vial load | E1 successful vial loads | Hodges–Lehmann shift with its Moses one-sided 95 % upper bound | 100 % power if the true cost is ≤ 4 s against a 5 s cap | Supported: correction is cheap enough to leave on. Not supported: its time cost is a trade-off to report | — (engineering trade-off) |
| Does the arm meet its repeatability claim? | **H4a**: unloaded RP ≤ 0.10 mm at all 5 poses | E3: 5 poses × 30 cycles, instrument *U* ≤ 0.025 mm | ISO 9283 RP; bootstrap-t bounds: 95 % upper to certify, 99 % lower per pose to refute, against an instrument-adjusted threshold | "Meets" 94 % at a true 0.07 mm, 5 % at 0.09 mm (instrument noise inflates RP); "certified" 89 % at 0.04 mm; an arm exactly at 0.10 mm is refuted 5.0 % of the time | Meets: the vendor figure holds here. Refuted: the error budget's 0.1 mm term is optimistic | The vendor's 0.1 mm specification |
| Does repeatability degrade with load and reach? | **H4b**, **H4c**: RP grows with payload; with reach | E3: × 2 payloads | Bootstrap bounds on the mean payload difference and the reach slope | 61 % and 83 % at 0.01 mm effects; 98–100 % at 0.02 mm | Supported: plan payloads and placements to stay near the base. Not supported: no detectable effect of that size | That a low-cost arm's repeatability is load- and reach-dependent |

**Outcome-neutral checks** (PROTOCOL.md §14.6): injected offsets verified in the commanded pose;
≥ 99 % tag detection at the look pose; operator–reviewer κ ≥ 0.8; E1 and E2 agree at zero offset;
instrument *U* ≤ 0.025 mm on a static target. A positive control runs in the pilot: 20 nominal
open-loop carrier loads must all succeed before counting starts.
